DEPI chambers

DEPI, the dynamic environmental photosynthesis imager, is a set of growth chambers that image the chlorophyll fluorescence of hundreds of plants many times a day, under light programs that can replay field conditions. DEPI was created in David Kramer’s lab at the MSU-DOE Plant Research Laboratory. Berkley Walker’s lab now runs it through C-PhLUX, the Center for Photosynthetic Light Use and Carbon Exchange, which is open to any MSU lab. I work on the DEPI software and analysis.

Recent work

What is new on DEPI 01

DEPI 01 is the first chamber to run the new control software. A run is now a plan of light, temperature, and image captures that the chamber checks against its limits, so a run can replay a heat wave as well as a sunny day. A web portal queues runs and returns readouts for each plant, and a color camera records a timelapse beside the fluorescence camera.

Experimental design

Letting one run choose the next

Some mutants look like wild type in constant light and only differ when the light changes. With Chih-Li Sung’s group in Statistics and Probability, we are building Bayesian experimental design into the chambers. Five days of light programs go in, and the model proposes the sixth, picking the program expected to separate a mutant from wild type the most.

If you have a mutant whose phenotype hides in constant light, or a project that could use DEPI, email me.

DEPI is described in Cruz et al. (2016), Dynamic environmental photosynthetic imaging reveals emergent phenotypes, Cell Systems 2: 365–377. C-PhLUX is part of the MSU-DOE Plant Research Laboratory.
