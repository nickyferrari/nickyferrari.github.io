Game of Life lab

Conway's Game of Life (1970) runs on four rules about how many neighbors a cell has, listed in full under the grid. Yet it contains gliders, guns, and universal computers. Draw on the grid, drop in a famous pattern, or change the rules themselves and watch a different universe unfold. The board starts loaded with a Gosper glider gun: press Run.

The rules, in full

Every cell looks at its eight neighbors, and all cells update at once:

  1. A live cell with fewer than two live neighbors dies. (underpopulation)
  2. A live cell with two or three live neighbors survives to the next generation.
  3. A live cell with more than three live neighbors dies. (overpopulation)
  4. A dead cell with exactly three live neighbors becomes live. (reproduction)

Rules 2 and 4 are the whole thing: survive on 2 or 3, born on exactly 3. That is what B3/S23 in the readout means, and it is why the rule editor in the panel only needs two rows of checkboxes. Every variant in the preset menu is the same four rules with different numbers in them.

Cells are tinted by age: bright blue for newborns, fading toward ink as they survive. The gun fires a glider every 30 generations. On a wrapping board its own gliders will eventually come back around and destroy it, which feels like a metaphor for something.
