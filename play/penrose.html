Penrose and multigrid tilings

The tiling is built from a grid of intersecting lines. Follow one line through both views, then change the number of directions to get more tile shapes.

…

Hover or tap a tile or grid line to follow its ribbon in both views. You can also use the direction and line controls. With a canvas focused, use arrow keys to change the line, Shift + arrow keys to change direction, or Escape to clear the highlight.

Appearance & export

Exports save the tiling with your current colors, zoom, and ribbon highlight.

How the grid makes the tiles

Every crossing in the grid creates a rhombus. Its two edge directions come from the two line families that cross there. Follow a line and its tiles join into a winding ribbon.

A tiling that never repeats

In a Penrose tiling, thick and thin tiles occur in proportions that approach the golden ratio. Small patches recur, but no translation makes the entire infinite tiling coincide with itself.

More than five directions

Five directions produce the two Penrose rhombi. More directions introduce more tile shapes. The pattern-shift control slides the line families relative to each other, changing how the tiles meet.

Inspired by MinutePhysics: Why Penrose Tiles Never Repeat. Based on de Bruijn’s multigrid construction. These are finite patches. Counts near the boundary depend on the patch size.
