About

I am a postdoctoral researcher in Berkley Walker’s lab at the MSU-DOE Plant Research Laboratory at Michigan State University, where I work on the software and analysis for the DEPI imaging chambers.

I earned my Ph.D. in Biochemistry at Louisiana State University in 2026, with a minor in Mathematics. My research in David Vinyard’s lab asked how algae adjust when the light around them keeps changing.

Before that, I worked with the RIPE project at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign on ways to improve photosynthesis in crops. My work combines hands-on experiments on plants and algae with the mathematics used to design and interpret them.

Research

DEPI imaging chambers

DEPI chambers image the photosynthesis of hundreds of plants under changing light. I work on the chamber software and analysis. With Chih-Li Sung’s group, we are adding experimental design that lets one run choose the light for the next. Read more on the DEPI page.

Photosynthesis under changing light

A regular flicker and a random one can deliver similar amounts of light. I study whether algae respond differently to the two patterns.

Making sense of overlapping signals

Several processes can appear in the same measurement. I develop ways to separate them and understand how the machinery of photosynthesis works.

Tools for measuring biology

I build tools for my own experiments, including programmable lights, optical hardware, and software for collecting and interpreting data.
